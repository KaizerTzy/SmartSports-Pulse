# Prompt 0 — Kickoff interview questions

Blog: SmartSports Pulse  
Candidate domain: smartsportspulse.com  
Artifact: interview only. No categories, keywords, sitemap, or strategy in this file.

Please answer in your own words. If a question does not apply, say so. If you are unsure, say what you *do* know rather than guessing.

---

## Audience

1. The starter describes the reader as “Korean beginner sports fans **and** bettors.” Are those the same person (a fan who is starting to bet), two different people you want equally, or is one the real priority and the other a nice-to-have?

2. When that reader lands on the site, what are they usually trying to do *in that moment*? (For example: understand a rule before a match, decode a number on an odds board, avoid a beginner mistake — name the actual job, not a demographic.)

3. Which sports, leagues, or events do they already follow? Please name only the ones that matter for this site. If “all sports” is the instinct, which 1–2 would you cut last if you had to launch narrow?

4. Who should **not** be treated as the reader? (For example: people hunting tonight’s picks, casino/slots players, high-volume traders, non-Korean speakers, minors.) What would make you say “this article is for the wrong person”?

## Purpose

5. In 90 days, “success” is an approachable Korean educational hub with beginner guides, odds breakdowns, and a working site. What would make **you** personally call that a win — a specific kind of article shipped, a feeling when you click around, something a friend could use, or something else? (Page-count goals are optional; do not invent a KPI if you do not have one.)

6. After about 10 minutes on the site, what should a first-time visitor be able to do or explain that they could not before? One or two outcomes is enough.

7. What is this site *for* in your world — a standalone project, support for another activity you already do, a search-traffic experiment, or something you have not decided yet? If it supports something else, say what that is without dressing it up.

## Voice

8. How should the writing feel? Name a tone you want (teacher, older sibling, calm analyst, etc.) and a tone you refuse (hype tout, slang-heavy tipster, stiff textbook, etc.).

9. Korean register: 존댓말 or 반말? How much betting slang is acceptable in body copy? Any English terms you want kept in English on purpose (and any you want always Koreanized)?

10. The brand name is English (SmartSports Pulse) and the candidate domain is English (`smartsportspulse.com`), while primary content is Korean. Is that intentional, and should the on-site name stay English, appear in Korean, or both? Do not invent a Korean brand name unless you already have one you want used.

## Topics

11. What 3–5 questions do beginners actually ask you (or keep searching) that this site should answer first? Write them as the reader would ask them, not as article titles.

12. Of **rules**, **odds explanation**, and **betting strategy**, which is the center of gravity, and which two are supporting? If you want all three equally, what would you drop if writing time is short?

13. Are match predictions, “who wins,” tips, or pick’em-style content in scope at all — even as examples inside an educational piece — or is the site strictly how-it-works / how-to-read / how-to-think?

14. Besides the title-keyword rule already given, which sports, bet types, or angles are off-limits in the *content itself*? Name them if you have them; do not add a “misc” bucket as a plan.

## Constraints

15. The never-list bans gambling keywords in **page titles** (e.g. bet, casino, slots, gambling), guaranteed-win/profit claims, and illegal site promotions. Does that ban also apply to URLs, meta descriptions, H1s, body copy, images, and internal link text — or titles only? Which Korean words, if any, are also banned in titles?

16. Publisher reality: which country are you operating from, and is this meant to be educational content only? Any age-gate, disclaimer, licensing, or “we do not take bets here” requirement you already know you must include?

17. Bookmaker names, odds screenshots, affiliate links, “where to bet,” or comparisons of betting sites: allowed, restricted (how?), or forbidden? Same question for promoting any specific platform.

18. What else would make you kill a draft on sight — even if it followed the never-list?

## Ops reality

19. Who actually writes, edits, and checks accuracy (you, a collaborator, a translator, a sports-odds person, AI-assisted drafts you rewrite)? If the honest answer is “me, in leftover time,” say that. A “complete library” in 90 days with no named writer is a vanity plan — what is the real throughput you can sustain (articles per week or per month)?

20. What already exists to work from (notes, old posts, spreadsheets, examples of odds you want explained), and what is still undecided (CMS, hosting, who hits publish, Korean proofreading)? Launch cadence after the first batch: burst then pause, steady weekly, or unknown until writing starts?
